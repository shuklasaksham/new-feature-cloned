01 · COVER
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  BRACKET   ◇   The AI decision workspace for client projects.

  A senior operator that sits between the client brief and the client
  document — so freelancers, small studios, and in-house designers stop
  losing time (and money) to fuzzy scope, ambiguous decisions, and
  back-and-forth review loops.

  Bracket is where you *decide*.

02 · PROBLEM — The invisible tax on every client project
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  WHO FEELS IT
  ──────────────
  • 1.6M+ freelance designers in India, ~59M globally on Upwork/Fiverr
  • 200k+ small design/creative studios (2–10 people) worldwide
  • Junior in-house product designers negotiating with PMs & engineers
  • Independent writers, photographers, video editors, illustrators

  THE SPECIFIC PAIN
  ─────────────────
  A "client message" arrives on WhatsApp, email, or Slack. It is vague,
  contradictory, and emotionally loaded. Between that message and a
  signed-off deliverable there is a hidden 4–12 hour tax per project:

    ─  Re-reading the brief 5x to find what actually matters
    ─  Framing the problem in your own words (usually mid-shower)
    ─  Making a call — pricing, scope, direction — without a peer
    ─  Writing a client-facing message that doesn't sound defensive
    ─  Handling revisions when the client "changes their mind"

  Missed one thing? Scope creep. Missed the client's real motivation?
  Rejection. Missed a risk? Unpaid rework. In India, the median
  freelance designer loses ~₹40,000/month to this tax alone.

  WHY EXISTING TOOLS FAIL
  ────────────────────────
  • Notion / Docs      → blank pages don't force clarity, they enable
                         waffling. No opinion, no push-back, no stance.
  • ChatGPT / Claude   → open-ended chat. No structure, no memory of
                         *this* project, no client-shareable output,
                         no push-back loop.
  • Figma / Miro       → visual thinking only. Doesn't help you decide
                         *before* the pixels.
  • Project management → Asana/Monday track *tasks* after the decision
                         is made. They don't help you make the call.
  • Traditional design → costs 6-figure ₹ and takes weeks. Not for the
    consultants           freelancer with three clients and a Tuesday
                          deadline.

  Nothing sits between "here's the mess" and "here's what I'll do."
  Bracket is that seat.


──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
03 · SOLUTION — A senior operator in five steps
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Bracket is not another chat wrapper. It's a **structured 5-step
  decision flow** designed by senior designers, wrapped in an AI that
  has an opinion and refuses to hand back a menu.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  01  UNDERSTAND      →  Paste the mess. Get the real frame.  │
    │  02  SIMPLIFY        →  Cut noise. Surface what drives calls.│
    │  03  STRATEGY        →  A stance, not a menu. With confidence│
    │                          score, alternatives, and risks.     │
    │  04  EXECUTION       →  Scope doc, client message, assumptions│
    │  05  REVIEW          →  Locked doc → client review link →    │
    │                          accept-with-signature or reject-    │
    │                          with-Bracket-drafted-suggestions.   │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  WHAT MAKES IT MEANINGFULLY BETTER (not just a prettier form)

  1. **Paste anything, land anywhere.**  Bracket reads WhatsApp threads,
     email chains, PDFs, screenshots. If the paste is rich enough, it
     auto-jumps you to Step 5 — a full sign-off document in ~25s. If
     thin, it asks 2–4 project-specific questions grounded in *your*
     brief (not templates).

  2. **A senior peer, not a yes-machine.**  Bracket's voice is calibrated
     across 5 personas (indie creative, small agency, in-house product,
     juniors levelling up, independent designers). It says "Pass — this
     brief is broken" when it should. It teaches juniors the reasoning.

  3. **The client is in the loop.**  Bracket-generated documents ship
     with a public review link. Clients accept-with-signature or reject-
     with-concerns. On rejection, Bracket drafts targeted response
     suggestions, maps each concern to the right step (1/2/3),
     regenerates only the affected steps, and emails the client an
     AI-drafted "here's what changed" note. Full loop closed.

  4. **Speed as a feature.**  Powered by Claude Haiku 4.5 — every step
     resolves in 6–15s. Full auto-build from a rich brief in ~25s.
     Whole document in under a coffee break.

  5. **Native mobile + PWA.**  Bracket installs on iOS/Android with
     push notifications ("Your project is ready ✓", "Client accepted
     ✓") and a mobile tab bar. Designed for the freelancer on a phone
     between meetings — not a desk-only tool.

04 · PRODUCT — Show, don't tell
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  FLAGSHIP FLOW (60-second demo)

  1. On dashboard, freelancer pastes: "Wedding planner wants a
     co-branded dessert table for a Goa wedding, 200 guests, ₹3L
     budget. Only Sujata can travel for 4 days. Worried about humidity
     ruining kunafa. Client mentioned Bombay Sweet Shop as reference."

  2. Subtle overlay: "BRACKET IS THINKING · Reading your brief…"
     → 25 seconds later, they land directly on Step 5.

  3. The document is ready:
     ─ Reframed problem: "You're scaling a home-studio operation into
        a logistics-heavy on-site production. The real risk isn't the
        recipes — it's transport and humidity."
     ─ Recommendation: "Curated excellence over scale — 5 signature
        items, Sujata on-site only, premium positioning."
     ─ 3 alternatives, 3 trade-offs, 4 risks with severity + mitigation
     ─ Scope doc, client message, assumptions, risk flags
     ─ Locked. Signed. One click → share with client.

  4. Client opens link on phone, taps "Accept + sign-off." Bracket
     pushes a mobile notification to the freelancer. Done.

  IF BRIEF IS THIN

  Bracket surfaces clickable follow-up questions specific to the
  paste — "Is this redesign of an existing page or from scratch?"
  "Looking at Bombay Sweet Shop, what specifically — visual style,
  layout, or interaction?" — tap to auto-insert into the paste box
  as Q/A stubs. User answers, Bracket re-runs.

  • Dashboard — projects list with stage tags, hero paste-box
  • Step 5 document — the sign-off view
  • Client review page — public URL, accept/reject flow
  • Rejection loop — AI-drafted response suggestions

06 · WHY NOW
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  1. **Claude Haiku 4.5 / GPT-5.2-class inference is finally cheap
     enough to embed in every step.**  In Jan 2024, the same 5-step
     flow would have cost ~₹85 per project run and taken 90 seconds.
     Today it costs ~₹7 and takes 25 seconds. That's the difference
     between a demo and a business.

  2. **The freelance economy passed critical mass.**  India alone
     added ~5M freelance creatives post-pandemic. They earn in INR,
     spend on tools in INR, and have been under-served by USD-priced
     Silicon Valley SaaS. Bracket is priced natively in ₹ from day one.

  3. **Designers now compete on decisions, not pixels.**  With Figma
     Make, v0, Midjourney, and Cursor, execution has been commoditised.
     What distinguishes a senior from a junior is *the call they make*
     before opening the canvas. This is the exact layer we own.

  4. **PWA + push notifications finally work on iOS (16.4+).**  We can
     ship a "native" mobile experience without an App Store review,
     without a native codebase, and with real push notifications to
     the freelancer's phone the moment a client accepts.

  5. **Behavioural shift: clients now expect written decisions.**
     Post-Zoom fatigue and remote-first hiring, both sides want
     receipts. "Send me a doc" is the new "let's hop on a call."

  OUR DURABLE ADVANTAGE (moat)

    1. **Persona-tuned voice as data**  — Our system prompt encodes
       5 personas × ~200 real client-project transcripts we hand-
       collected from Indian freelancers. This gets sharper with every
       user session. Off-the-shelf LLMs can't do this out of the box.

    2. **The client-loop is the retention flywheel**  — Every time a
       freelancer ships a Bracket document, a *client* clicks the link
       and sees Bracket's polish. That's an organic-acquisition
       surface competitors don't have. 1 freelancer × 8 clients/year
       = ~8 warm impressions of the brand.

    3. **INR-native pricing + PWA distribution**  — Silicon Valley SaaS
       won't drop below $19/mo. We win the ₹799/mo tier all day.

    4. **First-mover on the "decision layer" category**  — Notion owns
       docs. Figma owns canvas. Bracket owns the seat between input
       and output. Once we own that word ("decision workspace"), it's
       ours.
